The problem

Participation is graded from memory

That leaves students performing for attention and professors second-guessing themselves.

How it works

Three steps, no extra work in class

  1. Join.

    Students join the class Zoom from their own laptop, logged in with their university account and the Parti extension. Speakers stay muted, so there's no echo.

  2. Capture.

    When a student speaks, Zoom attributes it to their account. Parti writes a one-line summary of the contribution and tags it Question, Insight or Builds on others. No audio or full transcript is kept.

  3. Review and share.

    The professor adds their own notes and sets every grade. Students see their progress during the term and get a class summary to study from.

Clear limits

What Parti is, and what it isn't

Parti does

  • Report who contributed and what they said, in one line each
  • Jog the professor's memory
  • Give students feedback mid-term
  • Produce a study summary

Parti doesn't

  • Suggest or assign grades
  • Record audio or video
  • Recognize voices or faces (the camera only shows that a student is present)
  • Rank students publicly
Privacy first

Built to collect as little as possible

  • No voice or face recognition. Identity comes from the university login.
  • Only one-line summaries are kept, never audio.
  • The professor decides every grade.
  • Nothing is public. Students only see their own data.
  • Everything is deleted at the end of term.

Students are told exactly how Parti works before the first session.
